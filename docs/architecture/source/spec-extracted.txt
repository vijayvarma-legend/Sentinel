Core objective: Convert incoming supplier invoices into validated, auditable, policy-compliant ERP actions while keeping

calculations, policy enforcement, idempotency, and transaction safety.

1. Executive Summary & Core Objective
The Autonomous Accounts Payable & Invoice Exception Handler is an agentic enterprise workflow for processing
supplier invoices from ingestion through validation, exception resolution, human approval, and ERP posting. The system
is designed around a hybrid model: AI handles unstructured document understanding and exception reasoning, while
deterministic services enforce financial correctness.
Primary business outcomes
 Reduce manual invoice entry and exception handling.
 Increase straight-through processing for clean invoices.
 Detect duplicate, anomalous, and potentially fraudulent invoices.
 Apply configurable tolerance and approval policies.
 Provide auditable human approval for sensitive transactions.
 Prevent duplicate or unsafe ERP postings through idempotent execution.
 Measure extraction quality, agent decisions, latency, cost, and human overrides.
Design rule: No LLM output directly posts money. Every payment-impacting action passes through deterministic
validation, policy, authorization, and execution controls.
2. End-to-End Procure-to-Pay Workflow
The expanded workflow begins before document extraction and continues after an invoice decision so the system covers
the complete operational lifecycle.
Invoice Source → Ingestion → Vision Extraction → Deterministic Validation → Duplicate Detection →
Risk/Fraud Scoring → Exception Reasoning → Policy Engine → Auto-Resolution or HITL → ERP Execution →
Audit/Event Log → AgentOps & Evaluation

4. Core Modules & Responsibilities
4.1 Invoice Ingestion
 Accept invoices through email inboxes, REST APIs, file uploads, and batch imports.
 Normalize metadata, store the original document, assign a correlation ID, and calculate a document hash.
 Perform basic file validation and route unsupported or corrupted files to a dead-letter path.
4.2 Vision Extraction
 Process multi-page invoices and scanned documents using a vision-capable model.
 Extract supplier, invoice number, PO reference, line items, quantities, prices, taxes, totals, currency, and charges.
 Return structured Pydantic data with field-level confidence and source/page references where possible.
 Reject malformed or low-confidence payloads instead of silently passing them downstream.
4.3 Deterministic Validation Engine
 Retrieve PO and GRN records using controlled database tools.
 Perform line-level quantity, price, subtotal, tax, total, and tolerance calculations.
 Validate contract constraints and allowed charges where contract data is available.
 Keep mathematical and financial validation outside the LLM.
5. Matching, Tolerance & Validation Rules
The original strict three-way match is retained as the baseline, but the production system introduces configurable
tolerances rather than assuming every variance requires rejection.
Baseline: Invoice PO Reference == PO Number AND Invoice Quantity == GRN Quantity AND Invoice Price == PO Price

6. Duplicate Detection

Duplicate detection protects the AP workflow from repeated uploads, supplier resubmissions, or semantically identical
invoices with minor formatting changes.
 Exact document hash detection.
 Exact invoice-number + vendor matching.
 Fuzzy invoice-number normalization such as INV-8821 vs INV8821.
 Similarity checks using vendor, amount, PO, date, and line-item features.
 Route uncertain duplicates to HITL rather than automatically rejecting them.
Example: a document that differs visually but matches the same vendor, invoice number, amount, and PO should receive
a high duplicate score.
7. Risk & Fraud Scoring
A separate risk layer evaluates whether an otherwise processable invoice has suspicious characteristics. This is not a
claim that the system can prove fraud; it is a prioritization mechanism for review.
Signal
Example
Vendor anomaly
Invoice materially exceeds historical vendor amounts.
Price anomaly
Unit price is far outside historical or contracted range.
Payment-change risk
Bank account changed shortly before a large invoice.
Duplicate risk
Invoice closely resembles a previously processed invoice.
New vendor risk
First invoice from a vendor with limited history.
Behavioral anomaly
Unusual frequency, timing, amount, or charge pattern.
Illustrative risk output: Overall risk score 0.91, with duplicate risk 0.92, bank-change risk 0.98, vendor anomaly 0.84,
and price anomaly 0.71. The score should be explainable through the contributing signals.
8. Exception Reasoning Agent
The Exception Reasoning Agent receives deterministic discrepancy evidence and converts it into a structured
explanation and recommended action. It should not invent financial facts that are not present in the evidence.
Responsibilities
 Classify exceptions: price, quantity, tax, shipping, duplicate, contract, vendor, or other.
 Explain the discrepancy using PO, GRN, invoice, contract, and historical evidence.
 Recommend actions such as approve, request corrected invoice, request credit note, or escalate.
 Produce a structured action plan for the policy engine and HITL interface.
9. Policy Engine
Business policy should not be buried inside agent prompts. A dedicated policy engine makes approval thresholds,
tolerances, vendor restrictions, and escalation rules configurable.
Illustrative policy flow
Risk < 0.30 + all validations pass → Auto-process
Risk 0.30–0.70 or controlled variance → Review / conditional action
Risk > 0.70 or sensitive condition → Mandatory HITL
Any failed safety/idempotency check → Block execution
 Policies stored in PostgreSQL/configuration rather than hardcoded in agent logic.
 Version every policy so historical decisions can be reconstructed.

10. Human-in-the-Loop Dashboard
The HITL dashboard is the operational control surface for exceptions and high-risk transactions.

Human actions should be authenticated, role-based, timestamped, and written to the audit log. The interface should make
the evidence behind an agent recommendation visible before approval.
11. ERP Execution Layer
The ERP layer is intentionally separated from reasoning. Approved actions are converted into ERP-specific transactions
through an adapter interface.
Core workflow: Policy Approval → Authorization Check → Idempotency Check → ERP Adapter → Transaction
Verification → Audit Event
 Support a mock ERP first, followed by adapters for real enterprise systems.
 Use idempotency keys so retries cannot create duplicate financial transactions.
 Separate authorization from recommendation: an agent can recommend, but execution requires policy permission.
 Handle ERP timeouts and partial failures using retry and reconciliation workflows.
12. Auditability, Reliability & Security
 Persist immutable workflow events for every material decision.
 Store agent version, prompt/configuration version, model, tool calls, and decision evidence.
 Use correlation IDs across ingestion, LangGraph state, database operations, and ERP execution.
 Implement retries, exponential backoff, dead-letter handling, and recovery from interrupted workflows.
 Enforce RBAC for AP operators, managers, finance administrators, and system services.
 Protect invoice and vendor data through encryption, secrets management, and least-privilege database access.
 Never allow arbitrary LLM-generated SQL or unrestricted ERP commands.

15. Concrete Operational Example
Scenario: TechCorp issues PO #9901 for 10 laptops at $1,000/unit. The warehouse receives 9 units because one is
damaged. Supplier invoice INV-8821 bills 10 units at $1,050/unit plus an unexpected $200 shipping fee.
Stage
System behavior
Ingestion
Receives invoice PDF, stores original, assigns correlation ID and document hash.
Extraction
Extracts PO 9901, quantity 10, unit price $1,050, shipping $200.
Validation
Finds 10 billed vs 9 received; $1,050 vs $1,000 PO price; unapproved shipping.
Duplicate
Checks invoice number, vendor, amount, PO, hash, and similarity against history.
Risk
Scores unusual price/shipping and any relevant vendor/payment anomalies.
Reasoning
Classifies quantity, price, and unapproved-fee exceptions and proposes a resolution.
Policy
Determines which actions can be automated and which require manager approval.
HITL
Manager reviews evidence and approves/rejects the sensitive shipping decision if required.
ERP
Only after authorization and idempotency checks, posts the permitted transaction.
AuditOps
Records every decision and measures processing time, cost, outcome, and overrides.
The quantity variance can be calculated as 10 billed minus 9 received. The price variance can be calculated against the
accepted quantity. The shipping charge should be handled according to the configured contract/policy rather than
assumed to be valid or invalid.

18. Definition of Done / Success Criteria
 A valid invoice can travel from ingestion to ERP posting without manual intervention when all policies pass.
 An invoice with a price/quantity/tax/contract discrepancy is explained and routed correctly.
 A repeated invoice is detected without creating a duplicate payment.
 High-risk invoices are blocked or routed to authorized human reviewers.
 Every financial action is reproducible from the audit trail.
 ERP retries cannot create duplicate transactions.
 Model or prompt changes can be evaluated against a fixed benchmark.
 The system exposes measurable STP, accuracy, latency, cost, failure, and human-override metrics.
 The LLM cannot bypass deterministic validation, policy, authorization, or execution controls.
Final architectural principle: The goal is not to maximize the number of agents. The goal is to build a reliable
autonomous financial workflow in which agents are used where reasoning is valuable and deterministic software controls
everything that must be exact.
